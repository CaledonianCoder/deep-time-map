About the Deep Time Atlas

Each icon marks fossils found at that spot, plotted on today’s continents. Drag the timeline and the icons grow where a group is common in rocks of that age and shrink as it becomes rare. Sizes are relative to each group’s own peak, so a rare animal like Velociraptor still shows at full size where it is best known.

The time window is 5 million years wide. A fossil dated only loosely (for example “Late Cretaceous”, 34 million years) is spread across its whole range, so it counts for less at any one moment than a precisely dated find.

Fossil occurrences come from the Paleobiology Database (CC BY 4.0), via public exports in three open-source projects: petra (classified dinosaurs and birds), dinotime (reptiles) and the Heim lab’s PBDB_Scripts (about 650,000 animal occurrences across the last 540 million years). These are snapshots, so the newest finds are missing. Dinosaur names link to their pages on dinosaurpictures.org, which is built on the same database.
